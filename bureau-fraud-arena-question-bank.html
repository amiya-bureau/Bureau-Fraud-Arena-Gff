  • A message sent to the wrong number
  • A promotional message from a bill-payment app
WhyEvery scam needs you to act before you think, so it manufactures a deadline. Utilities send notices through your bill and their own app – not a panic SMS with a mobile number to call.

SF-A-L01-02

SF-A-L02-01
What is a 'fraud ring'?
  • A single fraudster who reoffends repeatedly
  • A group operating co-ordinated applications and accounts that look unrelated one at a time but share hidden links ✓
  • A category of high-risk merchants
  • A ring of stolen cards sold together on a marketplace
WhyThe word that matters is co-ordinated. A ring is not many frauds happening near each other – it is one operation wearing many faces, and the only place it exists is in the links.
Network intelligence / ring detection
SF-A-L02-02
Scam money almost never lands in one account and stops. What is a 'mule chain'?
  • A queue of victims targeted by the same scam script
  • A sequence of accounts the money is hopped through to break the trail before it is cashed out ✓
  • A list of blocked accounts shared between banks
  • A group of ATMs used for the same withdrawal
WhyLayering is the whole trick: each hop costs the fraudster almost nothing and costs an investigator a subpoena. Following the chain is how the money is actually traced, and blocking one link rarely stops it.

SF-B-L02-01

  • The age of an account
  • A blocked status flag
WhyFraud graphs are relationship databases. Edges encode the links — shared device, shared phone, shared beneficiary — that make a ring visible. Without edges, all you have is a list of individuals.

SF-B-L02-02

  • Sending money through a single high-value wire to a foreign account
  • Recruiting children under 18 as mule account holders
WhySmurfing defeats threshold-based transaction monitoring by keeping every individual amount below the level that triggers a report. It requires many accounts — which is exactly why mule networks exist.

SF-B-L02-07
A single shared IP address links twenty loan applications at one lender. Why might a risk team not treat this as conclusive evidence of a ring?

SF-B-L02-08
What is 'account takeover' (ATO), and how does it differ from opening a new mule account?

  • ATO requires physical access to a bank branch; mules operate only online
WhyATO is dangerous precisely because it skips the onboarding controls entirely — the account is already trusted and aged. Mule accounts have to be manufactured and cultivated. Both serve the same laundering function but arrive through different attack paths.
